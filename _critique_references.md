# Elite Website References — PropelBD v3

Scout: Fable (senior visual-design critic)  
Criteria: asymmetric editorial layout, kinetic type, restrained navy/blue palettes, premium B2B/agency feel.  
Filter: no Awwwards generic copy; only live, non-amateur pages with concrete, stealable decisions.

---

## 1. Linear — linear.app
**URL:** https://linear.app/

**Why it’s elite:** The landing page is essentially one centered headline split into word-tokens that stagger-fade in over a uniform near-black (#08090A) field; there is no hero image, no gradient text, and no decorative illustration. Everything below the fold—product screenshots, code snippets, issue cards—shares the same disciplined indigo/blue accent family, so the palette feels like a system, not a theme.

**Technique to steal:** Build the hero as a single statement in two or three lines, then reveal each word with a short Y-axis translation + opacity stagger. Use one blue only for primary CTAs and inline links; everything else stays in navy/off-white.

**PropelBD caveat:** Linear sits on near-black. For the brand lock, remap that to a deep navy (#0A1120-ish) rather than literal black.

## 2. DEPT — deptagency.com
**URL:** https://www.deptagency.com/

**Why it’s elite:** The hero is a compressed sans headline—“Invention at the intersection of marketing and technology”—set large with no supporting visual; the confidence comes from the type itself. Below it, a horizontal ticker of clients and a grid of oversized service cards with hover-reveal video thumbnails turn a B2B capability list into an editorial index rather than a brochure.

**Technique to steal:** Use one giant headline as the entire first viewport, then replace the standard three-equal-cards services block with a 2×2 or 3×2 index where each tile reveals motion or a color-shift on hover.

**PropelBD caveat:** DEPT’s background is mostly near-black (#121212) with a deep navy (#20105A) accent. Keep the structure, but anchor PropelBD in navy as the default surface.

## 3. R/GA — rga.com
**URL:** https://www.rga.com/

**Why it’s elite:** The page opens with a single centered statement in a dark field—“An independent creative innovation company for the intelligence age”—then immediately moves into a stacked work index where project names are display-sized and descriptors are tiny. The asymmetry is created through scale contrast and vertical rhythm, not columns, which is exactly what keeps it from looking like a template.

**Technique to steal:** Treat selected work as a typographic list: oversized project titles left-aligned, with a thumbnail and a one-line description appearing only on hover or at a smaller scale to the right.

## 4. Locomotive — locomotive.ca
**URL:** https://www.locomotive.ca/

**Why it’s elite:** Locomotive loads like a magazine spread: a massive serif headline (Editorial New) sits on the left, a cropped portrait or project image bleeds to the right, and a custom cursor scales into a ring over every interactive element. Scroll velocity subtly skews the canvas, so the page feels physically heavy without relying on neon glows or generic parallax clouds.

**Technique to steal:** Pair a high-contrast display serif with a neutral grotesque sans, offset the hero image from the type baseline, and add a custom cursor that enlarges over links/buttons. It instantly signals “studio” rather than “starter template.”

**PropelBD caveat:** Locomotive’s palette is often cream/charcoal. Remap to navy/off-white and keep the same layout logic.

## 5. Dogstudio — dogstudio.co
**URL:** https://www.dogstudio.co/

**Why it’s elite:** The hero deconstructs the phrase “We Make Good Shit” into four vertical columns of single characters that animate in sequence; the entire identity is carried by that kinetic type decision. The rest of the page is a dark, almost brutalist list of case studies with large imagery, which proves you can be playful and premium at the same time.

**Technique to steal:** Take a short manifesto headline and break it into character- or word-level vertical stacks, then reveal them on load with a stagger. This turns a generic value prop into a poster.

## 6. Cappen — cappen.com
**URL:** https://www.cappen.com/

**Why it’s elite:** The hero uses a custom animated glyph inside the headline “BUILDING TOMORROW FOR TODAY,” set against a deep navy field. Case studies follow in an asymmetric two-column grid where one column scrolls slightly faster than the other, creating depth without resorting to 3D hero illustrations.

**Technique to steal:** Replace the standard CTA button in the hero with a small, ownable animated mark or glyph, and let the case-study grid use differential scroll speeds to create editorial tension.

## 7. Hello Monday — hellomonday.com
**URL:** https://www.hellomonday.com/

**Why it’s elite:** Dark-mode canvas with oversized display type that responds to scroll and WebGL image distortions on project thumbnails. The type is unmistakably the hero; the imagery is held in a strict grid so the motion feels authored rather than chaotic.

**Technique to steal:** Use a scroll-linked transform on the hero headline (slight skew, scale, or horizontal drift) and keep project thumbnails in a clean grid with a hover distortion. The contrast between disciplined grid and fluid type is what makes it feel elite.

## 8. Vercel — vercel.com
**URL:** https://vercel.com/

**Why it’s elite:** Deep navy/black background, geometric sans, and a hero headline that animates in word by word—“Build agents on infrastructure that thinks like them.” Product UI cards float in with a subtle z-depth, but the color palette stays almost entirely monochrome with one electric blue for CTAs.

**Technique to steal:** Animate the hero headline as a sequence of 3–4 words, each fading/sliding in with a short stagger, and use floating UI cards or proof snippets at low opacity behind or beside the type.

## 9. Mercury — mercury.com
**URL:** https://mercury.com/

**Why it’s elite:** A fintech landing page that uses a dark navy default surface with warm off-white text, avoiding the startup cliché of bright gradients and cartoon illustrations. Product sections are editorial—large screenshots paired with sparse, direct copy—and the social proof is woven into the narrative instead of dumped into a logo bar.

**Technique to steal:** Make navy the default background, use a warm off-white (#F4F6FA or similar) for body copy instead of stark white, and let product imagery sit in full-width editorial bands rather than three-up feature cards.

## 10. Buck — buck.co
**URL:** https://www.buck.co/

**Why it’s elite:** The landing experience is an ambient showreel loop behind a minimal wordmark and project titles; the work itself becomes the texture, so there is no need for decorative backgrounds. The palette is dark and restrained, letting the motion in the showreel provide all the energy.

**Technique to steal:** If PropelBD has showreel or client footage, use it as a slow, muted ambient background behind a small amount of type, with a navy multiply/overlay wash so the footage never competes with the headline.

## 11. Area 17 — area17.com
**URL:** https://www.area17.com/

**Why it’s elite:** The homepage reads like a magazine spread: full-bleed project photography on one side, a minimal sans headline on the other, and whitespace used as an active compositional shape. Navigation is reduced to a wordmark and two or three links, which forces the layout to do the talking.

**Technique to steal:** Use an asymmetric 50/50 or 60/40 split in the hero—type on one side, full-bleed image on the other—and reduce the header to a wordmark plus a single CTA. The restraint is what sells the premium positioning.

## Summary: what to steal, what to skip

**Steal:**
- Single-statement heroes with word-by-word reveals.
- Navy as the default surface, one blue accent, warm off-white text.
- Asymmetry through scale contrast and editorial split-screen layouts, not three-equal-columns.
- Custom cursor, scroll skew, and hover-reveal media as the only “effects.”
- Project lists that behave like typographic indexes.

**Skip:**
- Pure black backgrounds (remap to deep navy).
- Gradient text on headlines.
- Neon glows, decorative 3D blobs, emoji, Unsplash fillers.
- Three-equal-card feature grids.

**Brand-lock check:** None of the references use gold (#C9A84C); none rely on fabricated metrics. Adapt their restraint to PropelBD’s navy/blue kinetic system and Dubai/Abu Dhabi focus.
